Methodology

The LEAR Framework

CLEAR-DST rates innovation readiness across four theoretically-anchored dimensions, each translated into concrete, project-level survey statements.

Leadership

Idea cultivation & psychological safety

Anchor: Edmondson (1999) — psychological safety theory. Leadership items ask whether leaders cultivate ideas, create safety to raise concerns, give clear direction, enable collaboration, give useful feedback, stay open to challenge, champion change, remove barriers, and support experimentation. 9 items.

Environment

Social capital between stakeholders

Anchor: Nahapiet & Ghoshal (1998) — structural social capital theory. Environment items assess collaboration, information sharing, early engagement, transparent decisions, trust and respect, contractual alignment, knowledge transfer, digital tool support, and conflict management between clients, consultants and contractors. 9 items.

Architecture

Structural ambidexterity

Anchor: O'Reilly & Tushman (2013) — organisational ambidexterity. Architecture items cover role clarity, governance effectiveness, decision timeliness, change-process clarity, escalation, interdisciplinary coordination, risk management, lessons-learned integration and information management. 9 items.

Resources

Organisational slack

Anchor: Nohria & Gulati (1996) — unabsorbed organisational slack. Resources items ask about time, staffing, technical expertise, specialist skills, budget flexibility, tool accessibility, training, technical support and surge capacity. 9 items.

Instrument Design

36 Items, Refined Through Pilot Testing

The full-scale instrument was refined from a larger pilot pool. Four items were removed where pilot analysis showed cross-dimension duplication or theoretical misfit — keeping each dimension conceptually distinct and each of the 36 remaining items pulling its own weight.

Section 2

Linking Culture to Cost

A second, 13-item module captures professional background and — where relevant — departure or retention experience. This isn't just demographic colour: it's the data that lets the report attribute turnover risk directly to a LEAR dimension.

Professional Profile

Role, sector, organisation size, country and experience — used to contextualise the report and, in the underlying cost model, to segment salary-band benchmarks.

Departure & Knowledge Loss

Primary and contributing reasons for leaving are mapped directly onto Leadership, Environment, Architecture or Resources — plus vacancy duration, ramp-up time and how much project knowledge was captured before someone left.
